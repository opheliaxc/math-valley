# New York State Mathematics Curriculum Structure — Grades 1–4

**Source:** New York State Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards (2017), Updated June 2019 — New York State Education Department (NYSED). This is the current, officially adopted set of standards for NYS. It is **not** the Common Core standards from another state, though it shares structure and much content with the original Common Core Math Standards on which it was built — NYS revised, clarified, and in some cases moved standards between grades in 2017 and again in the June 2019 update.

**About the coding system:** every standard uses the prefix `NY-` followed by grade, domain, and number (e.g., `NY-3.NBT.2`). This is the official NYSED designation, distinct from the plain Common Core code (`3.NBT.2`) used by other states. Some standards are split into lettered sub-parts (e.g., `NY-4.NF.3a`, `NY-4.NF.3b`) when NYS separated a single Common Core standard into distinct, separately assessable expectations — this is one of the clarifications NYS added in its revision.

**Legend used throughout:**
- 🆕 **New** — introduced for the first time at this grade
- 🔁 **Review/Fluency** — builds directly on a skill from the prior grade, often moving toward fluency or a larger number range
- ⏩ **Advances further** — this skill continues to grow in the next grade (noted so you can see where it's headed)

## Grade 1

### 1. Operations & Algebraic Thinking (NY-1.OA)

- **Represent and solve problems involving addition and subtraction**
  - 🔁 `NY-1.OA.1` — Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve one-step word problems (adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, comparing), with the unknown in any position. *(Prerequisite: NY-K.OA.2, addition/subtraction within 10.)*
  - 🆕 `NY-1.OA.2` — Solve word problems that add three whole numbers with a sum ≤ 20.
- **Understand and apply properties of operations and the relationship between addition and subtraction**
  - 🆕 `NY-1.OA.3` — Apply properties of operations (commutative, associative — informally) as strategies to add and subtract.
  - 🆕 `NY-1.OA.4` — Understand subtraction as an unknown-addend problem (e.g., solve 10 − 8 by thinking "8 + ? = 10").
- **Add and subtract within 20**
  - 🔁 `NY-1.OA.5` — Relate counting to addition and subtraction (e.g., counting on).
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-1.OA.6` — Add and subtract within 20 using strategies (counting on, making ten, decomposing, known-fact relationships); fluently add/subtract within 10.
- **Work with addition and subtraction equations**
  - 🆕 `NY-1.OA.7` — Understand the meaning of the equal sign; determine if addition/subtraction equations are true or false.
  - 🆕 `NY-1.OA.8` — Determine the unknown whole number in an addition or subtraction equation with the unknown in any position.

### 2. Number & Operations in Base Ten (NY-1.NBT)

- **Extend the counting sequence**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-1.NBT.1` — Count to 120 starting at any number less than 120; read and write numerals; represent a number of objects with a numeral. *(Prerequisite: NY-K.CC counting to 100.)*
- **Understand place value**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.NBT.2` — Understand a two-digit number's digits represent tens and ones (2a: 10 as a bundle of ten ones; 2b: 11–19 as a ten + ones; 2c: 10, 20…90 as tens).
  - 🆕 `NY-1.NBT.3` — Compare two two-digit numbers using >, =, <.
- **Use place value understanding and properties of operations to add and subtract**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.NBT.4` — Add within 100 (two-digit + one-digit, or two-digit + multiple of 10) using models/strategies based on place value.
  - 🆕 `NY-1.NBT.5` — Mentally find 10 more or 10 less than a two-digit number.
  - 🆕 `NY-1.NBT.6` — Subtract multiples of 10 from multiples of 10 (10–90).

### 3. Measurement & Data (NY-1.MD)

- **Measure lengths indirectly and by iterating length units**
  - 🆕 `NY-1.MD.1` — Order three objects by length; compare two objects' lengths indirectly using a third object.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.MD.2` — Measure an object's length using same-size length units laid end to end (no gaps/overlaps); express length as a whole number of units.
- **Tell and write time and money**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.MD.3a` — Tell and write time in hours and half-hours (analog and digital).
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.MD.3b` — Recognize and identify penny, nickel, dime, quarter and their values; use the ¢ symbol.
  - 🆕 `NY-1.MD.3c` — Count a mixed collection of dimes and pennies (total ≤ 100¢).
- **Represent and interpret data**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.MD.4` — Organize, represent, and interpret data with up to 3 categories; answer questions about totals and comparisons.

### 4. Geometry (NY-1.G)

- **Reason with shapes and their attributes**
  - 🆕 `NY-1.G.1` — Distinguish defining vs. non-defining attributes of shapes; build/draw shapes with defining attributes.
  - 🆕 `NY-1.G.2` — Compose 2-D or 3-D shapes to create a composite shape, and compose new shapes from that composite.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-1.G.3` — Partition circles and rectangles into two and four equal shares; describe using "halves," "fourths," "quarters."

## Grade 2

### 1. Operations & Algebraic Thinking (NY-2.OA)

- **Represent and solve problems involving addition and subtraction**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.OA.1a` — Use addition/subtraction within 100 to solve one-step word problems, all unknown positions. *(Prerequisite: NY-1.OA.1.)*
  - 🆕 `NY-2.OA.1b` — Use addition/subtraction within 100 to solve **two-step** word problems.
- **Add and subtract within 20**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.OA.2a` — Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.OA.2b` — Know from memory all sums of two one-digit numbers within 20.
- **Work with equal groups of objects to gain foundations for multiplication**
  - 🆕 `NY-2.OA.3a` — Determine whether a group of up to 20 objects has an odd or even number of members.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.OA.3b` — Write an equation to express an even number as the sum of two equal addends.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.OA.4` — Use addition to find the total in rectangular arrays (up to 5×5); write as a sum of equal addends. *(This is the direct bridge to Grade 3 multiplication.)*

### 2. Number & Operations in Base Ten (NY-2.NBT)

- **Understand place value**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.NBT.1` — Understand a three-digit number's digits represent hundreds, tens, ones (1a: 100 as ten tens; 1b: 100…900 as hundreds).
  - 🆕 `NY-2.NBT.2` — Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, 100s.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.NBT.3` — Read/write numbers to 1000 (numerals, number names, expanded form).
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.NBT.4` — Compare two three-digit numbers using >, =, <.
- **Use place value understanding and properties of operations to add and subtract**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.NBT.5` — Fluently add and subtract within 100.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.NBT.6` — Add up to four two-digit numbers.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.NBT.7a/7b` — Add and subtract within 1000 using models/strategies based on place value.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.NBT.8` — Mentally add/subtract 10 or 100 to/from a number 100–900.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.NBT.9` — Explain why addition/subtraction strategies work, using place value and properties.

### 3. Measurement & Data (NY-2.MD)

- **Measure and estimate lengths in standard units**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.MD.1` — Measure length to the nearest whole unit using rulers, yardsticks, meter sticks, tapes.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.MD.2` — Measure the same object with two different-sized units; compare results.
  - 🆕 `NY-2.MD.3` — Estimate lengths in inches, feet, centimeters, meters.
  - 🔁 `NY-2.MD.4` — Determine how much longer one object is than another.
- **Relate addition and subtraction to length**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.MD.5` — Use addition/subtraction within 100 to solve word problems involving lengths.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.MD.6` — Represent whole numbers as lengths on a number line; represent sums/differences within 100 on a number line.
- **Work with time and money**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.MD.7` — Tell and write time to the nearest 5 minutes, using a.m./p.m.
  - 🔁 `NY-2.MD.8a/8b` — Count a mixed collection of coins ≤ $1; solve real-world problems within $1 using ¢ notation.
- **Represent and interpret data**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.MD.9` — Generate measurement data; show it in a line plot.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.MD.10` — Draw a picture graph and bar graph (single-unit scale, up to 4 categories); solve simple comparison problems from the data.

### 4. Geometry (NY-2.G)

- **Reason with shapes and their attributes**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.G.1` — Classify 2-D figures as polygons or non-polygons.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-2.G.2` — Partition a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares; count the total. *(Direct bridge to Grade 3 area.)*
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-2.G.3` — Partition circles/rectangles into 2, 3, or 4 equal shares; describe as halves, thirds, fourths; recognize equal shares of identical wholes need not be the same shape.

## Grade 3

### 1. Operations & Algebraic Thinking (NY-3.OA)

- **Represent and solve problems involving multiplication and division**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.OA.1` — Interpret products of whole numbers (e.g., 5 × 7 as 5 groups of 7).
  - 🆕 `NY-3.OA.2` — Interpret whole-number quotients (partition into equal shares, or find number of equal groups).
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.OA.3` — Use multiplication/division within 100 to solve word problems (equal groups, arrays, measurement).
  - 🆕 `NY-3.OA.4` — Determine the unknown number in a multiplication/division equation relating three whole numbers.
- **Understand properties of multiplication and the relationship between multiplication and division**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.OA.5` — Apply properties of operations (commutative, associative, distributive) as multiplication/division strategies.
  - 🆕 `NY-3.OA.6` — Understand division as an unknown-factor problem.
- **Multiply and divide within 100**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.OA.7a/7b` — Fluently multiply/divide within 100; know all one-digit × one-digit products from memory.
- **Solve problems involving the four operations, and identify/extend patterns**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-3.OA.8a/8b` — Solve two-step word problems (all four operations); assess reasonableness using estimation/rounding.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-3.OA.9` — Identify and extend arithmetic patterns (e.g., in addition/multiplication tables).

### 2. Number & Operations in Base Ten (NY-3.NBT)

- **Use place value understanding and properties of operations to perform multi-digit arithmetic**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.NBT.1` — Round whole numbers to the nearest 10 or 100.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-3.NBT.2` — Fluently add and subtract within 1,000.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.NBT.3` — Multiply one-digit numbers by multiples of 10 (10–90).
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-3.NBT.4a/4b` — Understand a four-digit number's digits (thousands, hundreds, tens, ones); read/write in numerals, words, expanded form.

### 3. Number & Operations — Fractions (NY-3.NF)
*Note: Grade 3 fractions are limited to denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8.*

- **Develop understanding of fractions as numbers**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.NF.1` — Understand a unit fraction 1/b as one part of a whole partitioned into b equal parts; understand a/b as a of those parts. *(Prerequisite: NY-2.G.3, partitioning shapes into equal shares.)*
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.NF.2a/2b` — Understand and represent a fraction as a point/length on a number line.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.NF.3a–d` — Explain fraction equivalence; recognize/generate equivalent fractions; express whole numbers as fractions; compare fractions with the same numerator or denominator using >, =, <.

### 4. Measurement & Data (NY-3.MD)

- **Solve problems involving measurement and estimation of time, liquid volume, and mass**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.MD.1` — Tell/write time to the nearest minute; solve one-step elapsed-time word problems.
  - 🆕 `NY-3.MD.2a/2b` — Measure/estimate liquid volumes and masses (grams, kilograms, liters); solve one-step word problems.
- **Represent and interpret data**
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-3.MD.3` — Draw a scaled picture graph and bar graph; solve one/two-step comparison problems.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-3.MD.4` — Generate measurement data (rulers marked in halves/fourths of an inch); display in a line plot.
- **Geometric measurement: area**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.MD.5` — Recognize area as an attribute of plane figures; understand unit squares.
  - 🆕 `NY-3.MD.6` — Measure areas by counting unit squares.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.MD.7a–d` — Relate area to multiplication and addition: (a) find the area of a rectangle with whole-number sides by tiling and show it equals the product of the side lengths; (b) multiply side lengths to find areas in real-world and mathematical problems; (c) use tiling to show that area of a rectangle with sides a and b + c is a × b + a × c; (d) recognize area as additive and find areas of figures composed of non-overlapping rectangles (no more than one unknown side length).
- **Geometric measurement: perimeter**
  - 🆕 `NY-3.MD.8a/8b` — (a) Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving perimeters of polygons, including finding the perimeter given side lengths or finding one unknown side length given the perimeter and the other side lengths; (b) identify rectangles with the same perimeter and different areas, or the same area and different perimeters.

### 5. Geometry (NY-3.G)

- **Reason with shapes and their attributes**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-3.G.1` — Recognize and classify polygons based on the number of sides and vertices (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, and hexagons); identify shapes that do not belong to a given subcategory. Includes regular and irregular polygons; formal terminology is not required.
  - 🆕 `NY-3.G.2` — Partition shapes into parts with equal areas; express each part's area as a unit fraction of the whole (direct link to NY-3.NF.1).

## Grade 4

### 1. Operations & Algebraic Thinking (NY-4.OA)

- **Use the four operations with whole numbers to solve problems**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-4.OA.1` — Interpret a multiplication equation as a comparison (e.g., 35 = 5 × 7 means 35 is 5 times as many as 7).
  - 🆕 `NY-4.OA.2` — Multiply or divide to solve word problems involving multiplicative comparison.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-4.OA.3a/3b` — Solve multi-step word problems with whole numbers using all four operations, interpreting remainders; assess reasonableness using estimation.
- **Gain familiarity with factors and multiples**
  - 🆕 `NY-4.OA.4` — Find all factor pairs for a number 1–100; determine if a number is prime or composite.
- **Generate and analyze patterns**
  - 🔁 `NY-4.OA.5` — Generate a number or shape pattern that follows a given rule; identify features of the pattern not explicit in the rule itself.

### 2. Number & Operations in Base Ten (NY-4.NBT)
*Note: Grade 4 numbers extend to whole numbers up to 1,000,000.*

- **Generalize place value understanding for multi-digit whole numbers**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-4.NBT.1` — Recognize that in a multi-digit number, a digit represents 10× what it represents in the place to its right.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-4.NBT.2a/2b` — Read/write multi-digit numbers (numerals, names, expanded form); compare using >, =, <.
  - 🔁 `NY-4.NBT.3` — Round multi-digit whole numbers to any place.
- **Use place value understanding and properties of operations to perform multi-digit arithmetic**
  - 🔁 `NY-4.NBT.4` — Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.NBT.5` — Multiply a whole number of up to 4 digits by a 1-digit number, and multiply two two-digit numbers, using place-value strategies.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.NBT.6` — Find whole-number quotients and remainders (up to 4-digit dividend, 1-digit divisor) using place-value strategies.

### 3. Number & Operations — Fractions (NY-4.NF)
*Note: Grade 4 fractions extend to denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 100.*

- **Extend understanding of fraction equivalence and ordering**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-4.NF.1` — Explain why a/b = (n×a)/(n×b); use this to generate and recognize equivalent fractions.
  - 🔁 `NY-4.NF.2` — Compare two fractions with different numerators and denominators (common denominator/numerator, or benchmark comparison).
- **Build fractions from unit fractions**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-4.NF.3a–d` — Understand addition/subtraction of fractions with like denominators as joining/separating parts; decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions; add/subtract mixed numbers with like denominators; solve related word problems.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.NF.4a–c` — Understand a fraction a/b as a multiple of 1/b; multiply a fraction by a whole number; solve related word problems.
- **Understand decimal notation for fractions; compare decimals**
  - 🆕 `NY-4.NF.5` — Express a fraction with denominator 10 as an equivalent fraction with denominator 100; add two such fractions.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.NF.6` — Use decimal notation for fractions with denominators 10 or 100.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.NF.7` — Compare two decimals to hundredths by reasoning about their size.

### 4. Measurement & Data (NY-4.MD)

- **Solve problems involving measurement and conversion of measurements**
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-4.MD.1` — Know relative sizes of measurement units within one system (km/m/cm, kg/g, lb/oz, hr/min/sec, etc.); convert within a system.
  - 🔁⏩ `NY-4.MD.2a/2b` — Use the four operations to solve word problems involving distance, time, liquid volume, mass, and money (including simple fractions/decimals); represent quantities on a number-line diagram.
  - 🔁 `NY-4.MD.3` — Apply area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real-world problems.
- **Represent and interpret data**
  - 🔁 `NY-4.MD.4` — Make a line plot to display a data set of measurements in fractions of a unit (1/2, 1/4, 1/8); solve problems using the data.
- **Geometric measurement: angles**
  - 🆕 `NY-4.MD.5a/5b` — (a) Recognize that an angle is measured with reference to a circle centered at the common endpoint of the rays, by considering the fraction of the circular arc between the points where the rays intersect the circle; (b) recognize that an angle that turns through n one-degree angles has an angle measure of n degrees.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.MD.6` — Measure angles in whole-number degrees using a protractor; sketch angles of a given measure.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.MD.7` — Recognize angle measure as additive; solve addition/subtraction problems to find an unknown angle.

### 5. Geometry (NY-4.G)

- **Draw and identify lines and angles; classify shapes by properties of their lines and angles**
  - 🆕 `NY-4.G.1` — Draw points, lines, line segments, rays, angles (right, acute, obtuse), perpendicular and parallel lines; identify these in 2-D figures.
  - 🆕⏩ `NY-4.G.2a–c` — (a) Identify and name triangles based on angle size (right, obtuse, acute); (b) identify and name all quadrilaterals with 2 pairs of parallel sides as parallelograms; (c) identify and name all quadrilaterals with four right angles as rectangles.
  - 🆕 `NY-4.G.3` — Recognize a line of symmetry; identify line-symmetric figures and draw lines of symmetry.

## Grade 1–4 Progression Table

| Concept | Grade 1 | Grade 2 | Grade 3 | Grade 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Addition/subtraction — number range** | Within 20 (fluent within 10) | Within 100 (fluent), within 1000 (with models) | Within 1,000 (fluent) | Multi-digit, standard algorithm |
| **Multiplication/division** | Not introduced | Foundational: equal groups, arrays | 🆕 Introduced; fluent within 100 | Multi-digit × 1-digit, 2-digit × 2-digit; quotients with remainders |
| **Word problems** | 1-step, within 20 | 1-step then 2-step, within 100 | 2-step, all 4 operations | Multi-step, all 4 operations, remainder interpretation |
| **Place value** | Tens & ones (to 120) | Hundreds, tens, ones (to 1,000) | To 1,000s (4-digit numbers) | To 1,000,000; 10× relationship between places |
| **Fractions** | Halves/fourths (informal, via shapes) | Halves/thirds/fourths (via shapes) | 🆕 Formal introduction (denominators 2,3,4,6,8); number line, equivalence | Equivalence, comparison, addition/subtraction (like denominators), decimal notation (10ths/100ths) |
| **Measurement — length** | Nonstandard units | Standard units (in, ft, cm, m); number line | Estimation, mixed units | Unit conversion within a system |
| **Measurement — time** | Hour/half-hour | 5-minute increments | Nearest minute; elapsed time | Elapsed time in word problems |
| **Measurement — money** | Pennies/dimes (≤100¢) | Mixed coins (≤$1) | Not a separate focus | Embedded in multi-step word problems |
| **Geometry** | Attributes of shapes; partitioning into halves/fourths | Polygons vs. non-polygons; partitioning rectangles into arrays | Polygon classification by sides/vertices; partitioning shapes into equal areas (unit fractions) | Lines, angles, angle measurement, triangles by angle size, parallelograms and rectangles, line symmetry |
| **Area/Perimeter** | Not introduced | Foundational (arrays of squares) | 🆕 Introduced: area via unit squares & multiplication; perimeter | Applying area/perimeter formulas in real-world problems |
| **Data & graphs** | Up to 3 categories | Picture/bar graphs, line plots | Scaled graphs, line plots (fractional units) | Line plots with fractional data, used in problem solving |

### Skills introduced for the first time, by grade

- **Grade 1:** counting to 120; place value (tens/ones); equal sign meaning; partitioning shapes into halves/fourths; telling time to the half-hour; identifying coin values.
- **Grade 2:** three-digit place value; skip-counting; number line representation of sums; standard units of length; telling time to 5 minutes; picture/bar graphs; polygon classification.
- **Grade 3:** multiplication and division (concept, fluency, and properties); formal fractions as numbers; area (as a concept, tied to multiplication); perimeter; polygon classification by sides and vertices; liquid volume and mass units; elapsed time to the minute.
- **Grade 4:** multiplicative comparison; factors, multiples, prime/composite; multi-digit multiplication and division; fraction addition/subtraction and multiplication by whole numbers; decimal notation; angles and angle measurement; classification by geometric properties; line symmetry.

### Skills reviewed/built on from previous grades

- **Grade 2** reviews and extends Grade 1's addition/subtraction (moving from within 20 to within 100/1,000) and place value (tens/ones → hundreds/tens/ones).
- **Grade 3** reviews Grade 2's equal-groups/array thinking (NY-2.OA.4) as the direct foundation for multiplication, and reviews Grade 2's rectangle-partitioning (NY-2.G.2) as the foundation for area.
- **Grade 4** reviews Grade 3's fraction equivalence/comparison work, extending it to addition, subtraction, and multiplication by whole numbers, and reviews Grade 3's area/perimeter concepts, extending them to formula application.

### Skills that become more advanced in the next grade

- Addition/subtraction fluency range: 10 → 20 → 100 → 1,000 → multi-digit standard algorithm (Grades 1→2→2→3→4).
- Fraction denominators: none → informal halves/fourths → formal set {2,3,4,6,8} → extended set {2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,100} with decimal notation (Grades 1/2→3→4).
- Multiplication: equal-groups foundation → introduced & fluent within 100 → multi-digit (Grades 2→3→4).
- Time: hour/half-hour → 5-minute increments → nearest minute with elapsed-time problems → elapsed time embedded in multi-step word problems (Grades 1→2→3→4).
- Geometry: shape attributes & partitioning → polygon classification & array partitioning → polygon classification by sides/vertices & area-as-fraction → lines/angles, triangles by angle size, parallelograms/rectangles, symmetry (Grades 1→2→3→4).

## Notes on Recent NYS Standards Changes

The NYS Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards were adopted in **2017** and updated in **June 2019**. The June 2019 update clarified wording, added coherence linkages between grades, and in some cases split single standards into lettered sub-parts for clearer instruction and assessment (e.g., `NY-4.NF.3a–d`). These are the standards currently in effect and tested via the NYS Grades 3–8 assessments. There have been no further structural revisions to the P-4 standards since the 2019 update as of this writing — always check [nysed.gov/standards-instruction/mathematics](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/mathematics) for the current official version before finalizing curriculum content, since state standards can be revised.
